✍️ Judges’ Review: Care Essay Winning Entries
Myungshik Cho, Ph.D. in Philosophy

Reading these care essays led me to reflect earnestly once again on life in old age.
The rising proportion of the aging population presents a shared challenge of caregiving for all of us.
We have now entered an era in which care must go beyond the boundaries of families, caregivers, facilities, and robots, responding collaboratively as an organic community. The Chinese character for “person” (人) depicts two strokes leaning on one another to stand. Especially in later life, we must bring together all of our collective strengths to become a pillar of support for one another.

Based on vivid, firsthand experiences of care and nursing, the winning entries realistically depicted both the fulfillment and areas for improvement, making them deeply relatable. In particular, I was profoundly moved by how the authors endured countless difficulties in care settings with a strong sense of mission and actively proposed diverse alternatives to make better care possible.

In particular, the top-prize winning piece, “A Person Who Connects the Warmth of Care” by Soseul Park, stood out for its focus on researching care robots and applying them in the field—not merely to meet patients’ immediate needs, but out of a heartfelt desire to offer them a “better future.” We could clearly see the dedication and passion to propose programs and designs optimized for care environments and patient needs, continuously striving for improvement. It reminds us that for patients in various circumstances, we must move forward together as an organic cooperative community encompassing the touch of family, professional medical services, caregiver support, and the necessary robots and equipment.

Through your practice of care, your devoted efforts, and your proactive research aimed at improving the future, I believe that both patients and our own later years will become even more peaceful and joyful.

Thank you for sharing your sincere stories, and heartfelt congratulations to all the winners.
Thank you all for your wonderful work.

✍️ Judges’ Review: Robot Drawing Winning Entries
Myungshik Cho, Ph.D. in Philosophy

To children, robots seem to be warm and friendly companions, much like close friends.

The winning entries offer a glimpse into the thoughts and hearts of children as they relate to robots.
Across almost all submissions, one can feel how engagingly and enthusiastically the children view robots.

In particular, the Grand Prize winning work by Seojun Yang (age 5) delightfully portrays the joy of living in an environment alongside robots. First, the spatial composition is bold, and the relationship between the people and the robot is actively depicted. Even the facial expressions of both the robot and the people are drawn brightly, conveying the happiness of spending time together with a robot. Drawn with earnest effort through concrete observation and imagination, it has become a truly vibrant piece.

In the other winning artworks as well, each child depicted the robot they envisioned with warmth and affection, showing that children endow robots with personality and soul.
This fills us with anticipation for the rich experiences children and robots will build together as they commune in future educational settings.

Thank you for submitting such wonderful robot drawings, and sincere congratulations to all the winners.
Well done, everyone.
Thank you.
